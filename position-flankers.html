6 · 7 Flankers

Back Row · 6: Blindside — wide "1" forward · 7: Openside — breakdown threat

← LocksNumber 8 →

Your three things

Start here. If you can do these three, you are doing your job.

  1. Know which job is yours: 6 holds width in the 15m channel, 7 works inside the pod and goes to the breakdown.
  2. Tackle safely every time: both arms wrapped, head to the side.
  3. Arrive at rucks on your feet, stay legal, and get something.

Flankers cover more ground than anyone. The blindside holds the wide channel and carries hard. Hybrid athlete, elite pace and blends power with agility. The openside hunts the breakdown and never stops. Both are indispensable — the team's engine in every phase, while flankers are usually defined by their work rate.

New at U14. Your 1-3-3-1 roles are now clearly defined. The blindside (6) is one of the wide "1" forwards alongside the hooker. The openside (7) works inside the pods with the No.8. Your lineout positioning and pod coordination are also new at this level.

What you do

Open each one when you are ready. You do not need to learn it all at once.

Attack when we are set up (AMBER and GREEN)
  • 6 (BLINDSIDE): Wide "1" forward in the 15m channel alongside the hooker. You hold width and form a diamond with the wing and two backs. Core pod skills apply in your channel: carry into contact, tip left or right to the support runner, or pull back to the back at the base. Know which option is on before you receive.
  • 7 (OPENSIDE): Inside one of the pods with the No.8. You support the carrier or carry yourself as the tip of the diamond. Stay in close support before contact — ruck speed target is 3 seconds from ball to ground to 9's hands, and that only happens if you are arriving on your feet at the breakdown, legally, before the opposition seals.
  • GREEN zone: short carries, fast presentation, immediate recycling — convert the pressure into points.
Attack when the defence breaks open

When the defence breaks: 7 tracks the ball carrier's outside shoulder immediately — call your position and go. 6 gets width in the 15m channel and acts as the wide threat. Don't wait for the structure — use the disorganisation while it's there. Critical that you understand how easy the game can be if you let the ball do the work of stretching the defense by passing to team mates in space

Kicking and kick chase

Chase every kick — 6 from the wide channel inward, 7 from the inside out. Both converge on the receiver. Apply pressure, don't race the backs. Arrive controlled and on your feet.

Defence, zone by zone

RED zone: 7 first to every ruck, 6 locks the blindside channel — no overlap. AMBER: 7 hunts the breakdown, 6 is the outside defender in the wide channel. GREEN zone: 6 locks short side, 7 jackals legally. At every defensive breakdown — GET SOMETHING. 7: your jackalling is the most direct route to turnovers — legal technique, arrive on feet, stay over the ball. 6: your competitive carries at set piece slow their exit. Make them work for everything. Possession won back, time bought, or pressure created — all count.

Stretch: where you are building towards (Outstanding)

This takes time, and that is the point. Nobody is expected to be here yet.

  • 7 changes the game through breakdown work — wins penalties, forces turnovers, slows opposition ball.
  • 6 carries across the width of the field — the wide channel is a consistent attacking threat.
  • Reads the game a phase ahead — knows where the next breakdown will be before the tackle is made.
  • When the ball goes wide, one flanker supports and one holds the defensive inside — no gaps.
  • Communication leaders of the forward unit — set the pace and clarity of information.
Pathway lens: what pathway coaches may look for

The coaching team's understanding of what development-pathway coaches (Bath Rugby DPP) look at, at a high level. It is a guide, not a promise or a checklist.

  • Breakdown work rate — tackle count, ruck arrivals, and turnovers won are tracked. Be honest about yours.
  • Jackalling technique — legal body position and timing. Bath look for players who understand it, not just those who attempt it.
  • Versatility — can you play both 6 and 7 competently? Adaptability opens doors at the next level.
  • Communication — do you organise the people around you, or just do your own job well?
