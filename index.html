How we think about rugby

Our Game

Some of you are chasing a pathway place. Some of you come because your mates do. Both are welcome, and the same things matter for everyone: you play, you learn, and you want to come back next week.

🏉

What every boy should get

  • Game time. Everyone plays at least half of every match.
  • One clear job. You should always know your next job, and if you don't, ask.
  • Fun that is part of the plan. Games and challenges are how we teach, not a reward for finishing the drills.
  • Mistakes without a telling off. We coach what happens next, not what just went wrong.
🧭

How we coach

  • Behaviours as well as results. How you respond when it goes wrong tells us more than the scoreboard.
  • Decisions before game day. Nobody should be making a decision for the first time on the pitch.
  • Players talk and lead. Captains and senior players carry the message. We create the conditions.
  • Quiet, slow, deliberate in walk-throughs and before matches. Lower the noise, raise the attention.

The four things we look at

These apply to every player, every session, every game. The player who improves fastest is rarely the most talented one. It is usually the one who listens, tries things and keeps turning up.

1

Game Understanding

Knowing where to be, why you are there and what happens next. What's my next job? is the question to keep asking.

2

Behaviours & Coachability

Effort, attitude and communication. Listening, trying the correction, and asking for help when you need it.

3

Athletic Potential

Speed, stamina, power and movement. Everyone starts somewhere different. What counts is that you keep building.

4

Fundamental Skills

Catch and pass, carry, tackle and breakdown. Nothing else works if these don't, so we come back to them all season.

🔄

Why this year feels different

U14 is a deliberate step up. There are 15 players instead of 13, a full-size pitch, scrums with eight players, lineouts and a full kicking game. It all arrives at once, and it is normal for it to feel like a lot. Our job is to make the step feel manageable, exciting and built on good habits.

We want an intelligent, athletic team that plays heads-up rugby, rather than a team of big players who bulldoze. This year you will start to see moments where it all clicks. There will also be matches where it doesn't, and that is part of learning.

Our playing approach

The 1-3-3-1

One shape that works from anywhere on the pitch. Learn three ideas first. The detail is underneath if you want it, followed by your own position page.

1

Spread out

Between rucks the eight forwards spread across the width of the pitch instead of bunching around the ball.

2

Be patient

The pods pull defenders in. When the defence has committed, the ball goes to the space.

3

Rebuild fast

After every ruck the shape reforms. The quicker we reset, the more options we have.

GREENScoring end. Short carries, fast recycling, protect the gain line.
AMBERThe contest. The middle of the pitch, where the game is won.
REDDefensive end. Safe, simple, clear it.
The full walkthrough, phase by phase

What it is

The 1-3-3-1 describes how the eight forwards spread across the pitch between breakdowns. The hooker and the 6 are the two wide "1" forwards, holding the 15m channels. The other six (props, locks, the 7 and the No.8) form two pods of three inside. Each pod is a diamond with a back, the 10 or the 12, at its base. In each wide channel, the wide "1" forward forms a diamond with the wing and two backs. The 9 links the pods to the backs. The shape reforms after every ruck, not just from set piece.

Why it works

Protection in midfield: pods put bodies around the ball where defenders are most numerous. Decoy and misdirection: pods pull defenders inward so the ball can go wide. Symmetry: the shape is the same left and right, so we can switch the point of attack without reorganising. The defence sees the same shape every time and has to guess.

From a scrum or lineout

The shape cannot exist during a set piece because the forwards are in it. A starter play (a pre-agreed move) runs off the scrum or lineout with one purpose: make ground and create a ruck. As soon as the ruck forms, free forwards reset into their channels. After the set piece, props join whichever pod is on their side. The target is a ruck speed of 3 seconds from ball on the ground to ball in the 9's hands.

Phase play: the pods

Each pod has three options before the ball arrives: carry hard into contact, tip pass left or right to the support runner, or pull the ball back to the back at the base. The defence tells you which one is on, so everyone must know all three. The No.8 leads one pod from the front (the tip). The 7 works inside the pods. The pod's job is to cross the gain line, get fast ball and draw defenders inward.

The 9 and the backs

After every ruck the 9 can pass to the near pod (the 10 plugs into the base), pass to the 10 behind the first pod (the 12 then plugs into the second pod's base, or acts as a decoy), pick and run, or kick. The 10 can plug into the second pod's base and distribute, or run the backs off the back of the forward shape. The 9 calls the play to the pod before the ball arrives.

Attacking the edge

When the ball reaches the wide "1" forward, the wing and two backs form a diamond around them. The wing is the outer point and holds width to the touchline, ready to take the pass in space. The 13 reads the last defender: fix and pass, or take the gap. In the GREEN zone, if the 13 has fixed the last defender, the ball should already be in the wing's hands.

Defence

In the RED zone every forward is part of the defensive wall: guard/post at every ruck with the inside channel locked. In AMBER, arrive at every breakdown before the opposition can clear and step up on the snap. At every ruck, maul and set piece we GET SOMETHING: possession back, time won, or pressure on their 9 and 10. The 10 calls the line ("blitz" or "drift") before the opposition passes. The 12 is the trigger for the back three: until the 12 commits to carry, the wings stay deep. When the trigger fires, the near wing joins the line, the fullback slides across, and the far wing moves to fullback. This is the pendulum.

What changes from U13

Moving Up to U14

The five biggest changes and a full rule comparison, as published for 2025–26. RFU approved Regulation 15 changes for 2026–27, so check the current U14 Appendix 8 on englandrugby.com for anything that has moved.

1

15-a-side

Up from 13-a-side. More space, more positional responsibility for everyone.

2

Full-size pitch

100m × 70m. Roughly 50% more ground per player. Fitness matters from day one.

3

8-man scrum

Full 3-4-1 formation. #8 can pick up and run. Scrum-half can box kick from the base.

4

Lineouts begin

No lineouts at U13 — free pass from touch. At U14 uncontested lineouts begin. No lifting until U15.

5

Full kicking game

Box kicks, drop goals, and conversions all introduced at U14 for the first time.

Looking after yourself

Players & Parents

Short and practical. Boys this age are growing fast, so the basics matter more than any plan.

⚠
If in doubt, sit them out. Any player with a suspected head injury comes off straight away and does not return that day. Follow the RFU's HEADCASE guidance on englandrugby.com for the return-to-play steps. Tell a coach about any knock to the head, even if it seemed minor.
Six short guides: Getting readyYour routineSleepFood and drinkRecoveryStretching and yoga

🌙 Sleep

Sleep is when you recover, grow and lock in what you practised. Most boys this age do best on roughly 8 to 10 hours. A regular bedtime helps more than catching up at the weekend.

Go deeper →

💧 Water

Drink through the day, not only at training. Bring a full bottle and use every break. Pale yellow urine is a simple sign you are doing fine.

Go deeper →

🍽 Food

Regular meals with some carbohydrate (pasta, rice, bread, potatoes, fruit) and some protein (eggs, chicken, fish, dairy, beans). Eat a proper meal a couple of hours before training and something within an hour or so afterwards. No perfect diet is needed, just regular, varied food.

Go deeper →

🤸 Warm-up and cool-down

Warm up before every session and every match, with moving stretches such as leg swings, lunges and skips. Afterwards, easy jogging and gentle held stretches. If you are late, warm up first and then join in.

Go deeper →

🦵 If something hurts

Tell us. Growing boys often get sore knees, heels and hips, and pain that keeps coming back is worth getting checked. Don't play through it, and don't worry that telling us will cost you game time.

Go deeper →

💬 Worried about anything?

If you or your son are worried about food, weight, mood or anything else, talk to Martin or the coaching team, or your GP. Club welfare officer: [UPDATE: name and contact].
