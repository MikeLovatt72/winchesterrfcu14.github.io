Position guide · Back Three

11 · 14 Wings

Back Three · Width holders · Finishers · Pendulum unit · Kick chasers

← Fly-halfInside centre →

Your three things

Start here. If you can do these three, you are doing your job.

  1. Hold your width. Do not drift inside unless you are called in.
  2. Catch the high ball (call "mine" early) and chase every kick.
  3. Stay deep until the trigger (the 12 commits to carry). Tuck in when the ball is on the far side.

The wing who holds their width and sprints onto the ball in space scores tries. On a full-size pitch there is more space on the edges than any defence can cover — but only if you're in the right place when the ball arrives.

What you do

Open each one when you are ready. You do not need to learn it all at once.

Attack when we are set up (AMBER and GREEN)
  • You are the wide option in your channel's diamond — the outer point. When the ball reaches the wide "1" forward in your channel (hooker or blindside flanker), you and two backs form the diamond around them: you are the widest receiver, positioned to take the pass in space or act as the overlap that commits the last defender.
  • In the GREEN zone, you are the finisher — hold your width to the touchline, sprint onto the ball, and score. Do not drift inside and take away your own space, unless you are deliberately cutting inside to avoid running into touch. Space is not always on the outside. If the 13 has fixed the last defender, the ball should already be in your hands.
  • Learn to read the body language of your wide forward and how to run off their shoulders – both outside and inside.
Attack when the defence breaks open

When the defence breaks open and the ball reaches you, make a decision immediately — don't check back inside. If you have space, run. If the last defender is drifting, chip and chase. If you're isolated, go to ground cleanly and present. Chaos in the opposition is your best friend — you have the speed to exploit it if you commit instantly. You must be a support runner in the 15m channels, get on shoulders by being alert to these breaks and be the first to rucks.

Kicking and kick chase

KICK CHASE — your most important kicking role. When the 10 or 9 kicks, you chase from the widest position on your side, angling in toward the landing zone. Time your run so you arrive as the ball lands — not 5 seconds later. In the RED zone, if a kick comes to your channel and you have space, you may kick back — communicate to the fullback before you do. Give yourself time to grow into kicking the ball defensively. In the GREEN zone, do not kick — run.

Defence, zone by zone
  • When the ball is on the opposite side, tuck into covering position — this is the pendulum.
  • Do not enter the defensive line until the TRIGGER: ball reaches the 12 and it is clear they will not kick (committed to run or contact). When the trigger fires: join the line, fullback slides to your position, opposite wing moves to fullback coverage. Stay deep until the trigger — a kick over the top while you're in the line is a try against.
  • At every defensive ruck — your contribution is staying in position so the line stays wide and the 13 doesn't have to cover your channel. An organised back-three means the inside defenders can apply pressure at the breakdown without worrying about exposure on the outside. Your discipline in position is your defensive return.

Stretch: where you are building towards (Outstanding)

This takes time, and that is the point. Nobody is expected to be here yet.

  • Finishing in space is the team's most reliable try-scoring option — your pace in the corner is a planning point for every game.
  • A credible threat by coming in off the wing during set piece plays and can strike from any zone.
  • Counter-attack changes games — reads the defensive picture instantly and exploits it from deep.
  • Kick chase is among the best in the team — consistently converts pressure kicks into turnovers.
  • The pendulum never breaks down because of your communication with the 15 and opposite wing.
  • Defensive trigger read is precise — never in the line too early, never in the line too late.
Pathway lens: what pathway coaches may look for

The coaching team's understanding of what development-pathway coaches (Bath Rugby DPP) look at, at a high level. It is a guide, not a promise or a checklist.

  • Raw pace and footwork — acceleration in the first 10m and the ability to beat one defender. This is visible to scouts.
  • Kick receipt reliability — does a testing high ball unsettle you? Bath look for composure under aerial pressure.
  • Discipline in position — do you hold width even when the ball hasn't come to you for 10 phases?
  • Defensive trigger accuracy — do you read the 12's intent correctly and time your entry into the line?
