Position guide · Half Backs

10 Fly-Half

Half Backs · Backs organiser · First decision-maker · Kicking game owner

← Scrum-halfWings →

Your three things

Start here. If you can do these three, you are doing your job.

  1. Decide before the ball arrives. Calm and correct beats impressive.
  2. Kick by zone: RED long, AMBER tactical, GREEN keep the ball in hand.
  3. Call "blitz" or "drift" before the opposition passes.

The 10 makes the play. You receive from the 9, read the defence, and decide — and that decision must be made before the ball arrives. The best fly-halves at U14 remain calm, composed and make the right call, not the most impressive one. Don’t be afraid to make mistakes – then learn from them, the team will benefit from that. Essentially you drive urgency when needed but bring calm to the communications across the team – they will take their emotional direction from you.

New at U14. The full kicking game is available: box kicks, up-and-unders, cross-field kick pass, defensive clearance, chip & chase, penalties, conversions and drop goals. Where you kick is as important as how well you kick it — zone-based kicking decisions are now part of your game.

What you do

Open each one when you are ready. You do not need to learn it all at once.

Attack when we are set up (AMBER and GREEN)

You are the primary decision-maker for the backs within the 1-3-3-1. When the 9 passes to you (behind the first pod), you have two main options: (1) plug into the base of the second pod diamond yourself and distribute — the 12 becomes a decoy or support runner; (2) receive and run the backs off the back of the forward shape — 12 and 13 off your pass, or carry yourself as the strike option. The defence sees the same 1-3-3-1 shape every time. Your job is to read which element of that shape is under-defended and attack it. In the GREEN zone, if the opportunity is there — take it. Don't default to a kick when the defence is vulnerable.

Attack when the defence breaks open

When the structure breaks open, you become the primary decision-maker for the backs. If the opposition is disorganised, play fast — don't wait for the pods to reset. Pass wide immediately if the overlap is on. If you see a gap yourself, take it. The instinct to keep the ball moving in chaos is more valuable than waiting for the perfect shape.

Kicking and kick chase

RED zone: your kick exits the pressure — long, high, chased, ideally to touch. AMBER: kicking is a tactical choice — up-and-under to the corners, cross-field if the wing is unmarked, or grubber through the gap, chip and chase always a great option when momentum is on your side. GREEN zone: kicking is rarely right — two exceptions - one is a grubber into the corner when the defence is in line and two is cross-field kick pass when the wing has the space . Never kick without knowing where your chasers are and what the wind is doing.

Defence, zone by zone

You call the defensive system after every ruck — "blitz" or "drift" — and you must call it BEFORE the opposition passes. Your mark is the opposition 10. In drift, hold your channel. In blitz, step up on the snap AS A TEAM, you have to bring the team with you. RED zone: a missed call here costs a try — your communication is as important as your tackle. At every defensive ruck, your role is to ensure the forwards are getting something — time, pressure, possession. This is a “shotgun” call – need to develop the language with your Sh and Call "use it" to force the clock, call "blitz" to put pressure on their 9. Your voice at the breakdown is part of the defensive return, not just a signal for your back line.

Stretch: where you are building towards (Outstanding)

This takes time, and that is the point. Nobody is expected to be here yet.

  • Manages the game — reads what the team needs (go fast, slow down, kick, spread) and executes it.
  • Kicking game creates pressure through placement and variation, not just distance.
  • Your running threat changes the defensive picture — opponents genuinely worry about you carrying.
  • Starter plays from set piece are called and executed with precision.
  • Reads the game a phase ahead and communicates what's coming to the back line before it develops.
Pathway lens: what pathway coaches may look for

The coaching team's understanding of what development-pathway coaches (Bath Rugby DPP) look at, at a high level. It is a guide, not a promise or a checklist.

  • Decision-making speed — how quickly do you process the defensive picture and act? Bath look at this specifically.
  • Kicking technique — can you execute box kick, grubber, and cross-field under pressure, not just in drills?
  • Communication — does the back line function because of your calls? Or despite them?
  • Composure — how do you respond when a play breaks down? Reset and go again, or hesitate?
