Position guide · Back Three

15 Full-Back

Back Three · Pendulum organiser · Last line · Counter-attack launcher

← Outside centreAll positions →

Your three things

Start here. If you can do these three, you are doing your job.

  1. Catch the high ball. Call "mine", "yours" or "leave" every time.
  2. Be in the right place when the opposition kicks, with the back field covered.
  3. Organise the pendulum: do not slide across until the trigger fires.

You are the calmest player on the pitch. Everything that gets past the defensive line comes to you. The fullback who is always in position, who calls clearly, and who makes the right decision under the high ball is the player the whole back three depends on. Full-backs need to know where they are on the pitch at all times – especially where their 22 is when receiving kicks and returning on the full for touch.

New at U14. Receiving kicks on a full-size pitch with a full kicking game is a significant new challenge. You now organise the back-three pendulum — the coordinated movement of both wings around the ball. Counter-attack from deep is more important than ever.

What you do

Open each one when you are ready. You do not need to learn it all at once.

Attack when we are set up (AMBER and GREEN)
  • In the AMBER zone, you are the deep option in the wide channel diamond — you join the diamond behind the "1" forward and the wing, arriving from depth at pace. Your angle and timing must fix a defender — don't arrive flat, arrive running at the defense.
  • In the GREEN zone, you can join the attacking line from deep between the 10 and 12, or wide of the 13. Your arrival is the team's most unpredictable attacking option when the defence is set — because they have been watching the pods and the centres, and you arrive from a direction they weren't tracking.
Attack when the defence breaks open

When the defence is broken and a kick goes behind them, you read and react first. If you have space, go — your counter-attack from deep, running at a disorganised defence, is one of the highest-percentage attacking options in the game. Call loudly to the wings so they support your line, not their own instinct.

Kicking and kick chase

Your kicking game is critical in all three zones. RED zone: catching the kick and taking the “Mark!”. When relieving pressure - kicks are clear long and high from hand — touch if possible, or into the air for the chase. AMBER: kick to territory — cross-field to the far wing's channel or a long spiral to touch. GREEN zone: rarely kick, but a chip or grubber into the corner when the defence is in line is correct. You also organise the kick chase — call to the wings which side they cover before every opposition kick. Never catch a kick and stand still — decision first, then move.

Defence, zone by zone

You lead the back-three defensive unit. RED zone: you are the last line — catch everything, take the mark, communicate immediately, direct the kick or the carry. AMBER: call every kick receipt ("mine"/"yours"/"leave") and every wing covering position. When the ball reaches the 12 and the trigger fires (12 commits to carry), slide across to cover the near wing's vacated position — opposite wing moves to your position. Do not move until the trigger. Move early and the 10 kicks — you have left the back field exposed. If you are out of position from having supported the previous attack – your wing partners must recognize and respond – covering the red zone between them.

Stretch: where you are building towards (Outstanding)

This takes time, and that is the point. Nobody is expected to be here yet.

  • The back three functions as a unit because of your organisation — the pendulum is automatic, every phase.
  • Counter-attack changes games — reads the defensive structure from 50m and exploits it before it develops.
  • Joining the line from depth is the team's most unpredictable attacking option — because your timing is exact.
  • Kicking game from deep is varied, accurate, and always chased.
  • Never caught out of position — the back field is covered whatever else is happening.
  • The loudest and clearest voice in the back field — your communication makes every back- three player better.
Pathway lens: what pathway coaches may look for

The coaching team's understanding of what development-pathway coaches (Bath Rugby DPP) look at, at a high level. It is a guide, not a promise or a checklist.

  • High ball reliability under pressure — the first thing Bath look at for fullbacks. Can you catch it when it matters?
  • Counter-attack decision — did you read it correctly and early? Scouts watch your first two seconds after the catch.
  • Pendulum organisation — do the wings perform better because of you? Or do they work independently?
  • Kicking technique under pressure — can you clear effectively from inside your 22 with a defender in your face?
