9 Scrum-Half

Half Backs · Link between the pods and the backs · Game conductor

← Number 8Fly-half →

Your three things

Start here. If you can do these three, you are doing your job.

  1. Pass cleanly off both hands, with pace as well as accuracy.
  2. Tell the pod and the backs the play before the ball arrives.
  3. Call the defensive line after every ruck ("blitz", "drift" or "up") and pressure their 9.

The 9 touches the ball more than anyone and makes the first decision after every ruck. Your voice sets the tempo. Your pass speed determines whether the backs have time. When you're early and accurate, the whole team functions.

New at U14. Box kicks from scrums and lineouts are available for the first time. Your decision range is wider: pass to a pod, pass to 10, pick and run, or kick. You now link two separate units — the forward pods and the backs — and must communicate to both.

What you do

Open each one when you are ready. You do not need to learn it all at once.

Attack when we are set up (AMBER and GREEN)
  • You are the link between the 1-3-3-1 structure and the backs diamond. After every ruck, your options are: pass to the near pod (10 plugs into base), pass to the 10 behind the first pod (12 plugs into the second pod base or acts as decoy), pick and run yourself, or kick.
  • The 1-3-3-1 presents the same shape to the defence every time — your job is to read which option creates the best attacking picture and execute it before the defence resets. The earlier your pass, the more time the 10 has. The earlier your call to the pod, the more prepared the forwards are.
  • In the GREEN zone, control matters most — slower pass if needed, call the right play, don't force it.
Attack when the defence breaks open

If the defence is broken, go yourself or release immediately — the pick-and-snipe from the base is a genuine scoring threat. If the 10 calls for the ball and there's space on both sides, give it early and let them play. Don't wait for the structure to be perfect — if the opposition is in chaos, play fast.

Kicking and kick chase

Your box kick is a primary exit weapon from the RED zone and a pressure tool in AMBER. Execute it with height and hang time — your kick-chase forwards need time to arrive. In the GREEN zone, the box kick is rarely the right call — keep the ball in hand unless you are under extreme pressure. Never kick blindly; know where your chasers are before the ball leaves your boot.

Defence, zone by zone
  • RED zone: communicate the defensive call to the backs immediately after every ruck — "blitz", "drift", or "up". You are the link between forwards and backs. AMBER: stay tight to the ruck, be the inside defender after every pass. GREEN: if the ball is turned over, communicate to the back line before they lose their shape.
  • At every breakdown — your job is to pressurise the opposition 9. Your presence at the base, your speed off the mark after the tackle, your voice calling "use it" — all of it slows their recycling and buys your defensive line time to reset. Get something from every ruck.

Stretch: where you are building towards (Outstanding)

This takes time, and that is the point. Nobody is expected to be here yet.

  • The whole team knows the play before the ball arrives — because you told them. That is the job at its best.
  • Box kick is a controlled weapon — flight, height, and landing zone are all deliberate.
  • Controls tempo deliberately — accelerates when the defence is disorganised, slows when the team needs to reset.
  • Snipe from the base is a genuine scoring threat that the defence must prepare for.
  • The clearest and most consistent communicator on the pitch — your calls make everyone around you better.
  • Consistently faster ruck recycle than the opposition – works with the forward pack to deliver fast ball from the ruck averaging 3 seconds per ruck
Pathway lens: what pathway coaches may look for

The coaching team's understanding of what development-pathway coaches (Bath Rugby DPP) look at, at a high level. It is a guide, not a promise or a checklist.

  • Pass speed and accuracy — Bath scouts measure how quickly the ball leaves your hands and whether it arrives usable.
  • Decision quality under pressure — box kick vs carry vs pass. Are you choosing right when tired and behind?
  • Communication leadership — do the people around you perform better because of you?
  • Technical kicking development — box kick height, hang time, and consistent landing zone under pressure.
