8 Number 8

Back Row · Pod leader · Primary ball carrier · Back row anchor

← FlankersScrum-half →

Your three things

Start here. If you can do these three, you are doing your job.

  1. Make the call before the ball arrives: carry, tip pass, or pull-back to the base.
  2. Lead your pod: know your place at the front before every phase and tell your flankers what is on.
  3. When the pick-and-go is called, execute it cleanly and safely.

The No.8 leads the pod. You pick from the base of the scrum — something impossible at U13. When you make the right decision before the ball arrives, the whole attack flows. When you don't, it doesn't.

New at U14. You can now pick up from the scrum base and run — a major new option. Your pod leadership role is also new at this level. You work with both flankers as a three-man back-row unit that the 9 builds from.

What you do

Open each one when you are ready. You do not need to learn it all at once.

Attack when we are set up (AMBER and GREEN)
  • You lead one of the two pods. You sit at the front — the tip. Two forwards are behind you, and a back (10 or 12) plugs into the diamond base.
  • Core pod skills you must be comfortable with: carry hard into contact, tip pass left or right to your support runner immediately after contact, or pull the ball back to the back at the base. The decision is made before you receive — read the defensive picture as the 9 picks up and commit to the option that's on. Intelligent use of the feet to target soft shoulders.
  • Ruck speed is the standard you set for the whole pod. Target: 3 seconds from ball to ground to 9's hands. That requires your pod runners to arrive on their feet before the opposition seals — and it requires you to present cleanly so the cleanout is simple, not a battle.
Attack when the defence breaks open

G◈REEN zone: hard carry, clean present, fast recycle. You set up the try — someone else finishes it. When the defensive picture breaks open, decide immediately — carry yourself if the gap is there, or release the ball wide. Don't carry into a formed defence when space exists outside you. The fastest decision after chaos starts is usually the right one.

Kicking and kick chase

You don’t have a kicking role however your role when your team kicks defensively is to ensure the defensive line stays intact, fan outward, and pressurise the receiver. Start your chase before the kick lands — don't ball-watch, keep the line intact.

Defence, zone by zone

RED zone: guard/post every ruck, inside channel locked — nothing through the middle. AMBER: inside defender alongside the locks — step up aggressively on the snap of every ruck. GREEN: after a turnover, get between the opposition and your try line immediately. At every defensive ruck — GET SOMETHING. You set the standard. Make their 9 work, slow the ball, force an extra player in. A competitive breakdown buys time for your back line to reset. Nothing from a defensive ruck is the only unacceptable outcome.

Stretch: where you are building towards (Outstanding)

This takes time, and that is the point. Nobody is expected to be here yet.

  • The pod functions because of your decision-making — the right call is made before the ball arrives, every time.
  • Defence cannot predict when the pick is coming or where you'll go — you are genuinely unpredictable.
  • When you don't carry, your decoy run fixes a defender — the space you create is as valuable as the carries you make.
  • The 9 makes better decisions because you have already communicated what's on.
  • Sets the physical and behavioural standard for the whole forward unit.
Pathway lens: what pathway coaches may look for

The coaching team's understanding of what development-pathway coaches (Bath Rugby DPP) look at, at a high level. It is a guide, not a promise or a checklist.

  • Decision-making under fatigue — does your pick-and-go timing hold in the 50th minute? Bath watch late in matches.
  • Leadership quality — not just talking, but organising. Do your flankers know their jobs because of you?
  • Physical development — the No.8 role at U15 demands more. Are you building the base now?
  • Scrum understanding — do you know what's happening above you in the scrum and can you adapt to it?
