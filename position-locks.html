Position guide · Front Five

4 · 5 Locks

Front Five · 4: Lineout caller & pod forward · 5: Engine room & pod forward

← HookerFlankers →

Your three things

Start here. If you can do these three, you are doing your job.

  1. Bind correctly and drive straight in the 8-man scrum.
  2. Lineout: two-footed take-off, hands high and early.
  3. In the pod you are either the tip or the side support. Arrive at rucks on your feet and legal.

Locks are the heartbeat of the pack — primary scrum drivers, lineout specialists, and the relentless workers every phase needs. The best locks make every rep count.

New at U14. You're now in an 8-man scrum with normal binding permitted. Lineout jumping and calling are brand new — no lifting means timing your jump and the accuracy of the throw are everything. Your role inside the pods is also more structured than U13.

What you do

Open each one when you are ready. You do not need to learn it all at once.

Attack when we are set up (AMBER and GREEN)
  • After the set piece you join one of the two pods inside the 15-metre channels. The pod is a 3-man unit with a back at the diamond base (10 or 12 depending on which side the 9 passes to).
  • You are either the tip (carrying forward) or the side support. Core pod skills to be comfortable with: carry into contact, tip pass left or right to your support runner, or pull the ball back to the back at the base.
  • Read the defence before you receive — one of those three options will be on.
  • Ruck speed is critical. Target: 3 seconds from ball to ground to ball in the 9's hands. That means you are in close support before contact, arriving on your feet, and clearing legally — no penalties. In the GREEN zone, shorter carries and immediate presentation after contact — gain line first, distance second.
Attack when the defence breaks open

When the defence breaks, stop looking for the ruck — look for the space. Get on the shoulder of the ball carrier, call your position ("outside you!"), and support the carry into the gap. If you are in front of the ball, clear the channel — give the carrier a lane to run into.

Kicking and kick chase

Chase every kick — fan out from in to out. Arrive on the receiver with pressure, making the catch difficult and the counter-attack impossible. Stay onside — chasing offside gives them the ball back.

Defence, zone by zone

RED zone: inside channel, guard/post every ruck — nothing through the middle. AMBER: step up on the snap of every ruck, inside channel locked. GREEN: protect the gain line — a missed tackle here is extremely costly. At every ruck and set piece — GET SOMETHING. Win the ball back, slow their recycling, force their 9 under pressure, or make them commit extra players to secure possession. A competitive breakdown gives the team time, options, and momentum even without winning the ball.

Stretch: where you are building towards (Outstanding)

This takes time, and that is the point. Nobody is expected to be here yet.

  • The team's primary lineout platform — your consistency determines how many lineouts convert to attacking ball.
  • Leads the lineout unit — communicates adjustments to the hooker and calls alternatives when the primary option is covered.
  • Physical presence in carrying — defenders cannot simply absorb you and move on.
  • Scrum engine — drives the opposite lock backwards and generates momentum.
  • Tracks breakdowns that backs would reach before the opposition can seal.
  • Sets standards for effort and attitude. The pack looks to you. You lead your pod and demand more.
Pathway lens: what pathway coaches may look for

The coaching team's understanding of what development-pathway coaches (Bath Rugby DPP) look at, at a high level. It is a guide, not a promise or a checklist.

  • Lineout jumping technique — clean two-foot jump, strong hands, secure under pressure. This is easily observable and scouts watch closely.
  • Scrum engagement — do you understand what you're doing and why? Coachability on specifics is a differentiator at this age.
  • Work rate across a full 50-minute match — ruck arrivals and defensive tackle count tell the story.
  • Communication — are you organising the forward unit, or waiting to be told what to do?
