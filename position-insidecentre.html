12 Inside Centre

Centres · First receiver · Defensive line setter · Pod connector

← WingsOutside centre →

Your three things

Start here. If you can do these three, you are doing your job.

  1. Run onto the ball. Do not stand and catch it.
  2. You are the trigger for the wings: make kick or carry clear and early.
  3. Hold the inside shoulder of the opposition 10 in defence and call the line speed.

You receive from the 10 and make it count. A 12 running flat and fast onto the ball fixes defenders and creates the space the 13 exploits. When you go in hard, the whole back line has more to work with. 12’s are defined by hard, strong direct running that pulls defenses into them, great passing skills once that space has been created, good decision making, defensively solid and a work rate to match the No.7.

New at U14. The larger pitch creates more mid-field space to exploit. You are comfortable taking the ball at pace into heavy traffic and creating space for others. Defensive line speed — stepping up on the snap of every ruck — is now a genuine attacking tool. You are also the trigger point for the wings entering the defensive line.

What you do

Open each one when you are ready. You do not need to learn it all at once.

Attack when we are set up (AMBER and GREEN)
  • You are a key connector in the backs structure. When the 9 passes to the 10 and the 10 receives, you have two main roles: (1) act as a decoy runner (screen) for the pod — your run inside commits defenders and opens up the 10's options; (2) if the 10 passes to you, carry hard to fix the inside defender, then either offload to the 13 or go to ground cleanly.
  • You can also plug into the base of the second pod diamond — arriving behind the pod to distribute left or right from depth, making the shape unpredictable.
  • In the GREEN zone, your carry must be decisive — halfway measures in the 22 give the defence time to reset.
Attack when the defence breaks open

When the defence is broken, you are the primary ball-handler in the backs. If there is space outside you, release immediately — don't carry into a formed defence when the 13 or the wing has a clear run. If the opposition is scrambling, go yourself — your carry at pace is hardest to handle when defenders are out of position.

Kicking and kick chase

You are the TRIGGER for the wings. When you receive the ball and commit to a carry or contact, the near wing reads that as the signal to enter the defensive line. This means your decision — kick or carry — must be clear and early. If you are going to kick (chip, grubber, cross-field), communicate it before the ball arrives if possible, so the wing stays deep. If you carry and it is obvious you are not kicking, the wing enters the line.

Defence, zone by zone

You call the line speed from inside the backs: GREEN zone: one missed read here can concede a try — be loud and be right. At every defensive ruck — your line speed is the team's primary defensive weapon. A flat, fast defensive line forces their 9 to move the ball under pressure and limits the time their 10 has to make decisions. That is your contribution to getting something from every breakdown AMBER: step up aggressively on the snap, hold your channel (inside shoulder of the opposition 10), never drift off your mark. RED zone: communicate to the 9 and 10 immediately — your inside coverage stops anything through the middle – constantly calling for clean hits and clean ruck technique

Stretch: where you are building towards (Outstanding)

This takes time, and that is the point. Nobody is expected to be here yet.

  • Carries through the gain line to the second defender — commits numbers and creates phase opportunities.
  • Offloads out of contact — finds the 13 or back row in support at the right moment.
  • Defensive communication sets the pace of the whole back line — your call determines how the line moves.
  • The team's go-to when forward momentum is needed — you deliver it under pressure.
  • Your read of when to kick and when to carry is the trigger that keeps the wings in the right position.
Pathway lens: what pathway coaches may look for

The coaching team's understanding of what development-pathway coaches (Bath Rugby DPP) look at, at a high level. It is a guide, not a promise or a checklist.

  • Running lines — does your angle genuinely fix the inside defender? Bath assess whether the 12 commits the man.
  • Defensive line speed and accuracy — stepping up in the right system at the right time.
  • Carrying ability — gain line success rate. Can you consistently get over the gain line in contact?
  • Communication — do you organise the 13 and back line, or leave them to work it out?
