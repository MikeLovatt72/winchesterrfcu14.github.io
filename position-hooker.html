Position guide · Front Five

2 Hooker

Front Five · Lineout thrower · Set piece anchor · Wide 1 forward

← PropsLocks →

Your three things

Start here. If you can do these three, you are doing your job.

  1. Throw straight to the target, and practise it on your own because that is the only way it improves.
  2. Hold the 15m channel as a wide "1" forward and be a carrying threat. Know your option before you receive.
  3. Arrive at rucks knowing your role. After a bad throw, reset and deliver the next one cleanly.

Hooker is the hardest technical position in the team. You throw the lineout, hook in the scrum, and operate as a wide forward in the 1-3-3-1. There is no hiding place — and no position earns more respect when done well. Strong communicator! The best hookers are powerful in the scrum, can deliver lineout accuracy under pressure, is immensely powerful carrying in the 15m channel, leads defensively and brings people with them by leading through actions.

New at U14. You're throwing into a lineout for the first time. No lifting is permitted, so your throw must reach the jumper accurately — straight, timed, and on target. This is a skill that only improves through solo practice.

What you do

Open each one when you are ready. You do not need to learn it all at once.

Attack when we are set up (AMBER and GREEN)
  • After the set piece (line out), you are one of the wide "1" forwards — you hold position in the 15-metre channel. The diamond in your channel is built around you: forward anchor, with the wing and two backs (13 & 15, or whoever is available) shaping around you.
  • Your carry in the wide channel creates a genuine threat. When you carry: tip pass left or right to your support runner, or pull back to the back at the base of your diamond. Know which option is available before you receive the ball.
  • Ruck speed matters here too — be in close support before contact so your diamond can recycle in under 3 seconds.
Attack when the defence breaks open

When the defence is broken, get width and depth — don't follow the ball into the ruck. Arrive on the shoulder of the ball carrier and call for the pass. The wide channel is where you can do the most damage when the opposition is disorganised. Takes space when its presented and draws defenses in.

Kicking and kick chase

Chase all kicks — your chase line is from the 15-metre channel inward. You are pressuring the receiver, not racing the wings. If the ball is coming back to your channel, call for the catch early.

Defence, zone by zone

RED zone: inside channel locked, guard/post every ruck — exit via kick or tight forward carry. AMBER: defend from the 15-metre channel, work back to the ruck after each phase. GREEN: your lineout throw is part of the offensive platform — a poor throw here hands the opposition possession in their defensive third – giving them relief. At every breakdown — GET SOMETHING. Slow their ball, force an extra player in, pressure the 9. A competitive ruck is never wasted.

Stretch: where you are building towards (Outstanding)

This takes time, and that is the point. Nobody is expected to be here yet.

  • Lineout throwing is the team's reliable platform — call and delivery work seamlessly under pressure.
  • Adapts throw trajectory and timing to beat lineout defence without losing accuracy.
  • A genuine carrying threat in the 15-metre channel — opponents have to plan around you. Knows how to play with his wing partner and create space for him.
  • Leads front row communication in the scrum independently.
  • Actively works on throwing outside team sessions — owns this skill.
Pathway lens: what pathway coaches may look for

The coaching team's understanding of what development-pathway coaches (Bath Rugby DPP) look at, at a high level. It is a guide, not a promise or a checklist.

  • Throwing accuracy from 15m — Bath DPP scouts lineout reliability above almost everything else for hookers.
  • Willingness to practise alone — throwing only improves through repetition. Do you do it?
  • Front row technical understanding — can you explain what you're doing in the scrum and why?
  • Resilience — can you reset after a bad throw and deliver the next one cleanly?
